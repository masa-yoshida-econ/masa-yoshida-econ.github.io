## Climate Change and the Reallocation of Jobs

**1. Too Hot to Work? The Erosion of Male Comparative Advantage in Outdoor Jobs**

I document that men without college degrees increasingly work outdoors, as indoor job opportunities shrink. Rising temperatures drive labor force withdrawal by eroding their comparative advantage in outdoor jobs.

**2. Climate Change and the Decline of Labor Share**

We provide a novel hypothesis for the puzzle of the U.S. labor share decline since 2000: climate change speeds up labor-saving technological change (e.g., robot adoption), as capital may be more resistant to temperature than labor.

**3. Climate Change and Unemployment Dynamics: Evidence from U.S. Counties**

We show that climate exposure materially shapes monthly U.S. unemployment, but conventional seasonal adjustment masks the impact of climate change—even in the headline indicator guiding Federal Reserve policymaking and congressional debate.

## Immigration, Automation, and AI under Population Aging

**4. Immigration, Automation and the U.S. Cities: A Task-based Framework for Welfare Analysis**

Low-skilled immigrants compete not only with native workers but also with robots. In a dynamic spatial task-based model, immigration initially lowers native wages but raises them in the long run by slowing automation.

**5. Robots or Natives? Immigration Enforcement and the Reallocation of Dangerous Jobs**

Unauthorized immigration can sustain dangerous, labor-intensive jobs by slowing automation. Enforcement that removes immigrant workers may shift workplace injury risk onto natives rather than eliminate it by automation.

**6. Singularity and Seniority: Evidence from the Professional Hierarchy**

Using four decades of move-level data from professional shogi, we find that ICT shifted peak performance toward younger players, whereas AI reverses this trend—suggesting AI can narrow age gaps among knowledge workers by democratizing high-quality learning.

## Bargaining Theory and Experiments

**7. Using a Soft Deadline to Counter Monopoly**

Can buyers turn deadlines against a monopolist? A “soft” deadline—imperfect commitment to exit early—restores Coasian price pressure, leaving buyers better off and suppressing monopoly power.

**8. Deadline Credibility and Trade Efficiency**

Do all deadlines in one-on-one market transactions need to be perfectly credible? Theory and laboratory evidence show that an imperfectly credible earlier deadline can induce earlier concessions and improve trade efficiency.

## Work in Progress

**9. Climate Change and the Growth of Disability Rolls: Evidence from the US Counties**

The costs of extreme heat may persist long after a hot day ends. We study whether heat exposure raises disability insurance applications and awards, a lasting burden on social insurance.

**10. Too Hot to Marry? Rising Temperature and Declining Marriage among Millennials**

Millennials are the first generation to come of age in the new century, experiencing the most severe warming during their formative years. We study whether rising temperatures reduce marriage among less-educated young men by weakening their prospects for stable outdoor employment.
