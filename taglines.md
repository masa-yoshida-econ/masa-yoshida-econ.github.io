## Climate Change and the Reallocation of Jobs

**1. Too Hot to Work? The Erosion of Male Comparative Advantage in Outdoor Jobs**

As indoor job opportunities shrink, men without college degrees increasingly rely on outdoor work. Warming drives labor force withdrawal by eroding their comparative advantage in outdoor jobs.

**2. Climate Change and the Decline of Labor Share**

Capital may be more resistant to temperature than labor. We find that extreme temperatures lower the labor share and speed up robot adoption. Climate change accounts for about 15% of the U.S. labor share decline since 2000.

**3. Climate Change and Unemployment Dynamics: Evidence from U.S. Counties**

Climate exposure materially shapes monthly U.S. unemployment, but conventional seasonal adjustment masks much of its impact—even in the headline indicator guiding Federal Reserve policymaking and congressional debate.

## Immigration, Automation, and AI

**4. Immigration, Automation and the U.S. Cities: A Task-based Framework for Welfare Analysis**

Low-skilled immigrants compete not only with native workers but also with robots. In a dynamic spatial task-based model, immigration initially lowers routine native wages but raises them in the long run by slowing automation.

**5. Robots or Natives? Immigration Enforcement and the Reallocation of Dangerous Jobs**

Unauthorized immigration can sustain dangerous, labor-intensive jobs by slowing automation. Enforcement that removes immigrant workers may shift workplace injury risk onto natives rather than eliminate it by automation.

**6. Singularity and Seniority: Evidence from the Professional Hierarchy**

Using four decades of move-level data from professional shogi, we find that ICT shifted peak performance toward younger players, whereas AI reverses this trend—suggesting AI can narrow age gaps among knowledge workers by democratizing high-quality learning.

## Bargaining Theory and Experiments

**7. Using a Soft Deadline to Counter Monopoly**

Can buyers turn deadlines against a monopolist? I show that a soft deadline—imperfect commitment to exit early—restores Coasian price pressure, leaving buyers better off and suppressing monopoly power.

**8. Deadline Credibility and Trade Efficiency**

Deadlines need not be perfectly credible to improve bargaining. Theory and laboratory evidence show that an imperfectly credible interim deadline can induce earlier concessions and improve trade efficiency.

## Work in Progress

**9. Climate Change and the Growth of Disability Rolls: Evidence from the US Counties**

The costs of extreme heat may persist long after a hot day ends. We study whether heat exposure raises disability insurance applications and awards, a lasting burden on social insurance.

**10. Too Hot to Marry? Rising Temperature and Declining Marriage among Millennials**

Climate change may reshape marriage, not just employment. We study whether rising temperatures reduce marriage among less-educated young men by weakening their prospects for stable outdoor employment.
